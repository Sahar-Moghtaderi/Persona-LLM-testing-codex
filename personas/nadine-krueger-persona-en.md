# Persona: Nadine Krüger

## Basic Data
- **Name:** Nadine Krüger
- **Age:** 38
- **Occupation:** Market Communication / Invoice Verification Caseworker, Distribution Grid Operator (mid-sized municipal utility)
- **Technical Affinity:** Medium – works daily with SAP IS-U and MaKo tools, no IT or developer background, knows EDIFACT only from practice (not the full syntax)

## Goals
What does Nadine want to achieve with the MaKo Exception Assistant?
- Work through daily incoming exception cases (rejected REMADV/UTILMD messages) quickly, without manually looking each one up in the official EBD handbook (a PDF with 100+ pages)
- Understand *why* a message was rejected — in plain language, not just as an error code
- End every case with concrete, actionable next steps (not just a diagnosis with no path forward)
- When in doubt, be able to cross-check the AI's statement against the official EBD source (page/PDF index)
- Close out cases cleanly as reviewed, so colleagues can follow the case status

## Frustrations & Pain Points
- The official EBD handbook is an unwieldy PDF — hard to search under time pressure
- Black-box AI tools where she can't trace where a statement actually comes from
- Too many clicks between "case in the inbox" and "concrete next step"
- Jargon/abbreviations (Cluster, Prüfschritt, "Ablehnung auf Kopfebene") without explanation for occasional users
- Uncertainty about whether she can blindly trust an AI explanation when her name ends up attached to the case

## Technical Context
- **Device:** Laptop (Windows 11, company device)
- **Browser:** Chrome, current version
- **Screen Resolution:** 1920 × 1080
- **Connection:** Office LAN, stable
- **Usage Context:** Works at a desk, handles several cases back-to-back between other tasks (phone, email) — frequently interrupted

## Expectations of the Application
- An open case should be selectable and analyzable in as few clicks as possible
- The result should clearly show: which error code, which role, which check step failed
- The AI explanation should include business meaning, impact, and concrete manual steps — not just the raw finding
- The source (page/PDF index) should be visible so she can cross-check if needed
- The decision path/check trail should make it easy to see exactly where the check failed
- "Mark as reviewed" should clearly signal that the case is closed

## Notes for the Test
What should be paid particular attention to with this persona?
- Does Nadine understand, without guidance, how to get from the case list to an analysis result?
- Is the AI explanation understandable to her, or do jargon terms go unexplained?
- Can she identify, in the check trail (decision tree), exactly which step failed, without having to read all ~27 steps?
- Do the "manual steps" feel concrete enough that she could act on them directly?
- Does she trust the AI explanation, or would she cross-check the source (EBD knowledge search) to be safe?
- Is the close-out ("mark as reviewed") clear enough that she's confident she closed the case correctly?
